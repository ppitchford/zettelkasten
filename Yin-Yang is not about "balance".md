# Yin-Yang is not about "balance"
Yin-Yang is not about *good* versus *evil*.

Persian dualism and Christianity projected the combative nature onto the symbolism of yin-yang.

In traditional Chinese cosmology, Yin is night, winter, rest, stillness, the valley, and the moon. None of those are the “enemy” of their opposite; simply the other half.

Yin-Yang does not describe an equilibrium or balance to maintain, but a turning you cannot stop.

Yang originally meant the sunny side of a hill and Yin referred to the shady side of the same hill. One hill, two sides. As the sun moves across the sky the yin side becomes yang, and the yang becomes yin. Therefore it is better to described yin and yang as a location, position, or state that things pass through rather than a distinctive attribute or quality.

> *yi yin yi yang zhi wei dao*

“One Yin (dark _scīlicet_ aspect, form, side, part), one Yang (bright _scīlicet_ aspect, form, side, part) is called the Dao”. The Dao is not a balance of the two states, but a rhythm that moves through them. That is why the famous swirling symbol carries an opposite colored dot in each half. 

Yin and Yang are only mentioned once in the all of the *Daodejin*’s 5000 characters. The full theory comes from the *Yijing commentaries* and a separate philosophical school. This means that the concept of Yin-Yang was never a religious doctrine but an ancient Chinese mental model of how change works.

> *Know which season you are in and stop farming against the weather.*
