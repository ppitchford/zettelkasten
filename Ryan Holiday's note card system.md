# How to Remember Everything You Read

1. **Read with a pen.** Marginalia — agreement, objection, complaints about a lame metaphor. Reading as dialogue, books as consumables rather than artifacts. Explicit rejection of speed reading, via Marcus Aurelius on Rusticus: read attentively, don't settle for the gist, fewer books understood fully.
2. **Let it sit.** The finished book goes on a pile for weeks or months before extraction. The delay is a filter — much of what jumped out on the first pass doesn't survive the second.
3. **Extract onto cards.** 4×6, one item per card, with citation (author, title, page). Pliny: never read without taking extracts, and no book is so bad you can't take one or two things from it. The transcription is itself a rereading — a third pass through the material.
4. **Organize into a commonplace book.** Themed boxes; cards get moved, recategorized, rewritten as themes emerge. Separate commonplaces per project.
5. **Apply.** The stage he insists matters most: cards are building blocks for a chapter, a talk, a decision. Collection without use is failure.

> *Marginalia* refers to marks, notes, doodles, or illustrations written in the margins of a book or manuscript.

> To read attentively and not be satisfied with just getting the gist of it. —Roman Emperor Marcus Aurelius, *Meditations* (Book 1, Section 7)
